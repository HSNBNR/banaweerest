A few minutes of care each week keeps your bike safer, makes it last longer and saves you money on repairs. Heat, dust and sand are hard on bikes, so these habits matter even more here.

## 1. Check tyre pressure before every ride

Under-inflated tyres feel slow and puncture easily; over-inflated tyres lose grip. The recommended pressure range is printed on the side of every tyre. A floor pump with a gauge is one of the best investments you can make.

## 2. Keep the chain clean and lubricated

Dust and sand stick to oily chains and wear them out fast.

- Wipe the chain with a clean rag after dusty rides.
- Apply a small amount of **dry lube**, which suits hot, dusty conditions, to each link.
- Wipe off the excess. A chain should look almost dry on the outside.

## 3. Test your brakes

Squeeze each brake lever. It should feel firm and stop well before touching the handlebar. If you hear squealing or grinding, or the lever feels soft, have the pads and cables checked.

## 4. The "M-check"

Once a week, follow the shape of an "M" across your bike: front wheel and hub, fork and headset, handlebar and stem, saddle and seat post, pedals and cranks, and rear wheel. Look and feel for anything loose, cracked or worn.

## 5. Store it out of the sun

Direct sun breaks down tyres, grips and saddles. Store your bike indoors or under cover whenever you can.

## When to visit the workshop

- Gears that skip or will not shift cleanly
- Wheels that wobble or rub on the brakes
- Creaks or knocks you cannot track down
- Suspension that feels harsh or leaks oil
